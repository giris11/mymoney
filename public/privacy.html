Privacy

MyMoney — last updated 27 August 2026

MyMoney is a personal finance app that runs entirely in your web browser. It has no server, no user accounts and no login. This page describes exactly what it does with your information, because an app that handles money should be specific rather than reassuring.

Where your financial data lives

Your accounts, transactions, categories, budgets and settings are stored in your own browser's local database (IndexedDB), on the device you are using. They are not uploaded anywhere by default, and the developer of this app has no server, no database and no ability to see them.

Because the data is local to one browser, clearing that browser's site data deletes it. Exporting a backup file is the way to keep a copy.

Network requests the app makes

MyMoney makes no network requests at all except the following two, both optional and both off until you switch them on:

  • Exchange rates. When live rates are switched on, the app requests published daily reference rates from open.er-api.com (or latest.currency-api.pages.dev if the first is unavailable). These requests contain only a currency code such as GBP. No account, transaction or personal information is sent. This can be switched off in Settings → Rates, after which the app makes no network requests whatsoever.
  • Dropbox sync. When you connect sync, the app stores a copy of your database as a single file — mymoney-sync.json — in your own Dropbox, so that your other devices can read it. What that involves, precisely:
    • It goes into a folder of its own. The app is registered with Dropbox for App folder access, so Dropbox creates one folder for it under Apps/ and the permission the app holds reaches that folder and nothing else. The rest of your Dropbox is not merely off-limits to it: the app cannot list it, search it, or tell what is in it.
    • It asks for four permissions and no more: account_info.read (which Dropbox requires of every app), files.metadata.read, files.content.read and files.content.write. Sign-in is refused if it comes back with anything wider. Permission to share files, to write file metadata, or to read your contacts is deliberately never requested.
    • What leaves the device is the file, and nothing else. That file contains your accounts, transactions, categories, budgets, payees, tags, exchange rates and settings — the same content as a backup export — together with the name you gave the device and the time it was saved, so that another device can tell the two copies apart. It goes to Dropbox and nowhere else. No analytics, no error reports, no copy to the developer, who has no server to receive one and no access to your Dropbox.
    • Signing in. The app signs in through Dropbox's own window using PKCE, and holds no password and no secret of any kind. The sign-in token Dropbox returns is stored in this browser on this device only.
    • The file is yours. It stays in your Dropbox, under your control, and you can delete it at any time. The app never deletes it, not even when you disconnect.
    • Switched off in this build. Sync is currently disabled in the app itself, pending a review, and the Sync screen says so and offers no controls. Nothing has ever been sent to Dropbox. The description above is of what happens once it is switched back on.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, tracking, fingerprinting or advertising.
  • No cookies used for tracking.
  • No sharing or selling of information to anyone, for any purpose.
  • No account, email address or password is collected, because there is no login.

Revoking access to your Dropbox

You can disconnect sync inside the app at any time, under Settings → Sync. That tells Dropbox to cancel the permission and forgets the sign-in stored on that device.

To revoke it from Dropbox's own side — everywhere, on every device, immediately — visit dropbox.com/account/connected_apps, find MyMoney, and disconnect it.

Neither of those deletes the sync file. To remove the data as well, delete mymoney-sync.json from the app's folder in your Dropbox, then delete it permanently from your Dropbox deleted files.
